What Is a Share? · 2 / 13

What is a share, really?

Imagine a pizza company sells 100 slices of itself, worth $100,000 total. If you buy one slice, you own 1% of that company — that slice is a share. Tap below to see how owning more changes things.

A $100,000 company, split into 100 slices ($1,000/slice)

Tap both options to see how ownership scales with slices.
🤝

You own a real piece

A share isn't a coupon or a bet — it's actual partial ownership of the company that issued it.

📈

You can profit two ways

If the company grows, your slice can be worth more. Some companies also pay owners a cut of profits, called a dividend.

📉

You can lose money too

If the company struggles, your slice can be worth less than you paid. Owning a share is never a guarantee.

Myth or Fact · 8 / 13

Myth or fact?

Three things beginners get wrong about the market. Tap your answer for each.

You need a lot of money to start investing.

Myth. Many platforms let you buy fractional shares for just a few dollars — you don't need to buy a "whole" expensive share.

You have to perfectly time the market to come out ahead.

Myth. Even professionals struggle to time short-term swings. Most long-term investors just invest consistently and hold.

A single stock is generally riskier than a broad index fund.

Fact. One company can struggle or fail. A broad index fund spreads that risk across hundreds of companies at once.

Recap · 12 / 13

Here's what you now know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓The stock market is a marketplace for buying and selling small ownership pieces of real companies.
✓Prices move on supply and demand — more buyers than sellers pushes prices up, and vice versa.
✓Stocks, bonds, and funds are the three basic building blocks you can put in your basket.
✓Index funds spread your money across hundreds of companies, smoothing out the ride.
✓Avoid the traps: hot tips, hourly checking, and putting too much in one company.
✓Investing consistently — and automatically — beats trying to time the market.
Your next move: set up a small automatic monthly investment into a diversified fund. That's how good investing habits build themselves.
