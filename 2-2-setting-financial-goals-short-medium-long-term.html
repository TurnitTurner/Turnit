Myth or Fact · 7 / 12

Myth or fact?

Three common beliefs about goal-setting. Tap your answer for each.

You need a high income before long-term goals are even worth setting.

Myth. Time and consistency matter more than the size of your income — small, regular contributions started early often outperform larger ones started late.

Writing a goal down and giving it a real deadline makes you meaningfully more likely to follow through.

Fact. A specific number and date turns a vague wish into something you can actually plan and measure progress against.

Every goal, regardless of timeline, should use the exact same investment strategy.

Myth. A goal three months away and one thirty years away call for very different levels of risk — timeline should always shape the strategy.

Recap · 11 / 12

Here's what you now know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓The same dollar goal needs very different monthly savings depending on your timeline.
✓A real goal is SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound.
✓Every goal fits Short-Term (0-1yr), Medium-Term (1-5yrs), or Long-Term (5+yrs).
✓Where you keep the money — and how much risk it takes — should match that timeline.
✓Reviewing goals yearly and giving each one a real priority beats chasing all of them at once.
✓Automating the split across goals means progress happens whether or not you think about it.
Your next move: write down one short, one medium, and one long-term goal — with a real number and date attached.
