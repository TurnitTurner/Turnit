Myth Or Fact · 8 / 13

Myth Or Fact?

Six common beliefs about goal-setting. Tap your answer for each — the explanation appears right after.

You need a high income before long-term goals are even worth setting.

Myth. Time and consistency matter more than the size of your income — small, regular contributions started early often outperform larger ones started late.

Writing a goal down and giving it a real deadline makes you meaningfully more likely to follow through.

Fact. A specific number and date turns a vague wish into something you can plan around and measure progress against.

Every goal, regardless of timeline, should use the exact same investment strategy.

Myth. A goal three months away and one thirty years away call for very different levels of risk — the timeline should always shape the strategy.

Adjusting a goal as your life changes is good planning, not failure.

Fact. Raises, moves, and new priorities all change what's realistic. A goal you revisit once a year stays useful; one you never revisit quietly goes stale.

Funding two or three goals well usually beats spreading yourself across ten at once.

Fact. The same monthly amount split ten ways moves every goal slowly. Prioritizing a few means you actually reach them, which builds momentum for the rest.

Money for a goal two years away belongs in the stock market so it grows faster.

Myth. Two years isn't enough runway to recover from a downturn. If the market dips right before your deadline, you're forced to either delay the goal or take the loss.

Recap · 12 / 13

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓The same dollar goal needs very different monthly savings depending on your timeline.
✓A real goal is SMART: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-Bound.
✓Every goal fits Short-Term (0-1 year), Medium-Term (1-5 years), or Long-Term (5+ years).
✓Where you keep the money — and how much risk it takes — should match that timeline.
✓Reviewing goals yearly and giving each one a real priority beats chasing all of them at once.
✓Automating the split across goals means progress happens whether or not you think about it.
Your next move: write down one short, one medium, and one long-term goal — each with a real number and date attached.
