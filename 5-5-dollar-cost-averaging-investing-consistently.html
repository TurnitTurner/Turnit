Recap · 8 / 9

Here's what you now know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓Dollar-cost averaging means investing a fixed amount on a regular schedule, no matter the price.
✓The same fixed amount automatically buys more shares when prices dip, fewer when they rise.
✓In a rising market, a lump sum invested sooner often ends up ahead — DCA isn't automatically superior.
✓DCA spreads out the risk of unlucky timing, which can matter as much as the math.
✓A regular paycheck contribution is already dollar-cost averaging, without any extra effort.
✓For a lump sum already in hand, investing it sooner is often the better-performing choice on average.
Your next move: if you're investing from a paycheck, you're already doing this — the goal now is just consistency.
