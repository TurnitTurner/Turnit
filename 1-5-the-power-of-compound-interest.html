Myth Or Fact · 7 / 10

Myth Or Fact?

Eight statements about compounding — some true, some the exact thing people get wrong. Tap your answer on each, in any order, and the reasoning appears underneath.

Compound interest only really matters if you're starting with a lot of money.

Myth. Compounding works on small amounts too — starting early with modest contributions often beats starting late with more money.

The earlier you start, the more time compounding has to work in your favor.

Fact. Time is the single biggest lever in the compounding formula — a head start of even a few years can be hard to catch up to later.

Interest is only ever calculated on your original deposit, never on interest you've already earned.

Myth. That describes simple interest. Compound interest is calculated on your original deposit plus all the interest it has already earned — that is the whole engine.

Compounding can work against you too — a credit card balance compounds in exactly the same way.

Fact. The same maths that grows an investment grows a balance you owe. That is why carrying a card balance month to month gets expensive so quickly.

Skipping contributions in your twenties is easy to make up later by contributing more in your fifties.

Myth. Every dollar you skipped early had decades left to compound. A dollar added at fifty has far less time, so catching up usually costs several times what the gap saved you.

A higher rate of return always beats a longer time horizon.

Myth. Not usually. Time compounds every single year, and a rate advantage has to hold for decades to overcome someone else's head start — while the higher rate is the part you control least.

Reinvesting dividends and interest, rather than taking them as cash, speeds compounding up.

Fact. Each reinvested payout buys more, and that more goes on to earn its own growth. Taking the payout as cash quietly removes it from the engine.

Compound growth looks slow at first and then accelerates.

Fact. Early on, growth is small next to what you are putting in. The curve steepens later — which is exactly why so many people quit before the interesting part.

Recap · 9 / 10

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓Compound interest earns interest on interest, not just on your original deposit — that's what makes it snowball.
✓Four ingredients drive it: principal, rate, time, and consistency — and time usually matters most.
✓The Rule of 72 gives you a quick way to estimate how long it takes money to double at a given rate.
✓Panic-selling during a downturn locks in losses and stops the engine — staying invested lets it recover and keep compounding.
✓Reinvesting and staying consistent, even through dips, keeps the snowball rolling.
✓The biggest risk to compounding usually isn't the market — it's interrupting it.
Your next move: let your contributions run on autopilot, especially when the market gets rocky. That's when compounding needs you the most.
