Recap · 9 / 10

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓The annual IRA contribution limit is shared across a Roth and a Traditional combined, not one allowance for each.
✓You can never contribute more than you actually earned that year, no matter what the limit says.
✓There is no minimum or maximum age to contribute, as long as you have earned income.
✓Age 59½ is the line for penalty-free withdrawals. Before it, expect income tax plus a penalty of about 10%.
✓Traditional IRAs require RMDs starting around age 73. Roth IRAs do not, during your lifetime.
✓A standard brokerage account has none of these rules — no limits, no age thresholds, ever.
Your next move: look up this year's actual IRS limit before you contribute — then move on to choosing and opening your first account in the next lesson.
