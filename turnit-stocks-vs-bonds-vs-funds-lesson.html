Myth or Fact · 8 / 10

Myth or fact?

Tap your answer for each.

A bond means you own part of the company that issued it.

Myth. A bond means the company owes you money — you're a lender, not an owner.

Mutual funds and ETFs can both hold a mix of many different investments.

Fact. Both structures can bundle stocks, bonds, or a mix of both into a single purchase.

Bonds are always 100% risk-free.

Myth. A borrower can still fail to pay you back — bonds are generally steadier than stocks, not risk-free.

An ETF can be bought and sold throughout the trading day, just like a stock.

Fact. Unlike a mutual fund's once-a-day price, an ETF trades continuously all day long.

Recap · 9 / 10

Here's what you now know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓A stock means you own a piece of a company. A bond means you lent it money.
✓Every bond has a principal, an interest rate, and a maturity date.
✓A single fund purchase can hold dozens or thousands of stocks and bonds at once.
✓Mutual funds price once a day; ETFs trade and re-price all day long, like a stock.
✓Stocks generally swing more with higher potential; bonds are generally steadier with less upside.
✓A fund's overall risk just depends on what mix of stocks and bonds it actually holds.
Your next move: next time you hear "fund," ask yourself what's actually inside it.
