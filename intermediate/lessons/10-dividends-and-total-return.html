The Long Run · 4 / 8

So Why Does It Matter?

Because each reinvested distribution earns on its own from then on. $10,000 in once, two settings.

The gap between them is the whole lesson.
And In Real Life, 1960–2025

The S&P 500 compounded at 10.48% a year with dividends reinvested against 7.44% on price alone — dividends supplied roughly a third of the annual total return. $10,000 left alone became about $7.19 million reinvested, or roughly $1.36 million with every payment taken as cash.

Myth Or Fact · 5 / 8

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims you will hear about dividends. Tap your answer.

A dividend is extra money on top of what your shares are worth.

Myth. On the ex-dividend date the price typically falls by about the payment. The money moved from the company to you; it was not created.

Price return understates what a long-term holder earned.

Fact. Headline index numbers are usually price return. Over 1960–2025 that was 7.44% a year against a total return of 10.48% — a gap that compounds into millions.

Buying just before the ex-dividend date gets you free income.

Myth. You pay a price that still includes the payment, then watch it drop out. You end up with the same value split differently — and in a taxable account, a tax bill you did not need.

Reinvesting is a setting, not a decision you make each quarter.

Fact. A DRIP runs automatically once switched on, and most brokerages let you set it per holding. It is one of the few places where doing nothing is the active choice.

A higher yield means a better investment.

Myth. Yield is the payment divided by the price, so a falling price pushes it up. Some of the highest yields on the market belong to companies in visible trouble.

Recap · 7 / 8

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓Once a DRIP is switched on, reinvesting happens without you — a notification is usually the only sign.
✓A dividend moves money between pockets. On the ex-dividend date the price drops by roughly the payment.
✓Reinvesting adds nothing today. It works because every future distribution is calculated on a bigger holding.
✓Price return is the headline number; total return is the one that counts what you were paid.
✓Over 1960–2025 that was 7.44% a year against 10.48% — dividends were roughly a third of the result.
✓A high yield is not a good deal. It is often just a price that fell.
Your next move: open your brokerage and check whether reinvestment is on. In Robinhood it's under Account → Investing. Plenty of people find theirs has been off the whole time.
