Myth Or Fact · 5 / 8

Myth Or Fact?

Five things people conclude from a fund page. Tap your answer.

A bigger fund is a better fund.

Myth. Net assets tell you how popular a fund is and how easily it trades. VOO holding over a trillion dollars says a great deal about its liquidity and nothing whatsoever about its future returns.

An ETF's price can differ from what its holdings are worth.

Fact. That is the premium or discount. On a fund this heavily traded it is usually hundredths of a percent; on a thin or hard-to-trade fund it is worth a look before you buy.

The yield shown on the page is what you'll earn.

Myth. Dividend yield is the income paid out over a recent stretch, and it shifts as prices and payouts change. It is one slice of your return, not the whole of it.

Two funds tracking the same index show similar numbers.

Fact. Same index, so near-identical holdings and concentration. Where they part company is the fee, the inception date and how long they have had to prove it.

A fund that lags its index by about its fee is broken.

Myth. That is a fund doing its job. A tracking difference of roughly the expense ratio is the expected result — it is a fund beating its index by a wide margin that should make you curious.

What's Missing · 6 / 8

Three Things It Can't Tell You

It is complete about the fund and silent about you. Open each one.

Whether It Fits You

Every number describes the fund — its net assets, its fee, its holdings. None of them knows your timeline, your taxes, or what you already hold.

What It Will Return

The performance chart is the field most people read first and should weigh least. It starts at the inception date — a day chosen by the fund company, not by you.

What You Already Own

A fund page cannot see your other funds. Two pages can both look excellent and hold almost the same companies — which is the next lesson.

Recap · 7 / 8

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓Every fund page carries the same handful of fields, and you can read all of them.
✓Net assets measure popularity and ease of trading, never quality.
✓Dividend yield is recent income — a slice of your return, not the whole.
✓The inception date sets the window for every "since inception" number.
✓A good index fund trails its index by roughly its fee. That tracking difference is the design working.
✓The page is silent on what matters most: whether it suits you, what it will return, and what you already own.
Your next move: open the page for a fund you hold and find all eight fields. Two minutes, and most people have never done it once.
