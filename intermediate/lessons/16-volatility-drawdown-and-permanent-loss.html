Myth Or Fact · 6 / 9

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims you'll hear about market drops. Tap your answer.

A 20% drop in your portfolio means you've permanently lost 20% of your money.

Myth. That's a drawdown, not a permanent loss — nothing is locked in until you actually sell.

The S&P 500 has recovered from every drawdown in its history, eventually.

Fact. True so far, including the 34% COVID crash and the 57% 2008 crisis — though "eventually" has meant anywhere from six months to over five years.

Volatility and the risk of permanent loss are basically the same thing.

Myth. A diversified fund can be volatile and still fundamentally sound. Permanent loss is a different question — whether the value is actually gone for good.

Selling during a drawdown is what turns a temporary paper loss into a permanent one.

Fact. The decline only becomes permanent for you at the moment you sell and lock in that price.

A single company's stock carries the same risk of permanent loss as a broad index fund.

Myth. A single company can go to zero, as Lehman Brothers did in 2008. A fund spreading risk across hundreds of companies is far less exposed to any one of them failing.

Recap · 8 / 9

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓Volatility is normal, constant price movement, usually triggered by news or world events — not a warning sign by itself.
✓A drawdown is a decline from a recent peak. It's a paper loss until you actually sell.
✓A permanent loss is capital that's genuinely gone — locked in by selling, or because the investment itself was wiped out.
✓The S&P 500 fell 34% in early 2020 and recovered to a new high just six months later — a severe drawdown, not a permanent loss.
✓Selling during a drawdown is what converts it into a permanent loss.
✓A diversified fund isn't immune to drawdowns, but it's far less exposed than a single company to permanent loss.
Your next move: before your next big drop, decide now what you'll do. A plan made in advance is harder to panic out of than one made in the moment.
