Myth Or Fact · 6 / 8

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims. Tap your answer.

If you leave before you're vested, you lose everything in the account.

Myth, and it is the single most common misunderstanding here. Vesting only ever applies to employer money. Every dollar of your own elective deferral, and everything it has earned, is yours from day one.

Asking for a cheque instead of a direct rollover triggers withholding even if you intend to roll it over.

Fact. 20% must be withheld from a taxable distribution paid to you, whatever you plan to do next. You then have 60 days to roll it over — and to roll the full amount you have to make up that 20% out of your own pocket.

Workplace plans are always cheaper than an IRA because of their scale.

Myth. Large plans often are. Small ones frequently are not, because the cost of running the plan is spread across fewer people. It is worth comparing the actual numbers rather than assuming either way.

Your own contribution limit is separate from what your employer puts in.

Fact. For 2026 you can defer $24,500 of your own pay, with $8,000 more from age 50 and $11,250 instead at ages 60 to 63. Employer money sits outside that limit, under a separate and much higher combined cap.

Cashing out a small balance is no big deal — it's only a few thousand dollars.

Myth. The tax and the penalty are the small part. The expensive part is the decades of compounding you just cancelled, which is exactly why this decision is worth more thought at 25 than at 55.

Recap · 7 / 8

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓Your own elective deferral is always 100% yours. Vesting only ever applies to the employer match.
✓The slowest schedule the law allows reaches 100% at six years — but many plans are far faster, and only your plan documents say which.
✓Plan fees sit on top of fund expenses, in a disclosure you have already been sent.
✓A direct rollover moves money provider to provider with nothing withheld and nothing taxed.
✓Take a cheque instead and 20% is withheld on the spot, with 60 days to replace it if you still want to roll the full amount over.
✓Cashing out early costs tax, a 10% penalty, and — far more — every year of compounding that money would have had.
Your next move: find your plan's vesting schedule and its fee disclosure. Both are in your plan documents, and most people have never read either.
