Myth Or Fact · 7 / 13

Myth Or Fact?

Six claims about allocation. Tap your answer.

Owning more funds always means owning a wider mix.

Myth. Two funds holding the same companies leave you exactly as concentrated as one. What matters is whether the holdings differ, not how many tickers you own.

A single total market fund is already an asset allocation.

Fact. It's 100% stocks, weighted by company size. That is a deliberate allocation — most people just never wrote it down.

Your stock-to-bond split shapes your results more than which company runs the fund.

Fact. For most long-term investors the mix drives far more of the outcome than the provider. Two broad funds tracking the same index behave almost identically; 90% stocks and 30% stocks do not.

A 100% stock mix is automatically right for anyone with a long timeline.

Myth. A long runway makes an equity-heavy mix workable, not automatic. The mix also has to be one you can hold through a deep fall — a plan you abandon at the bottom is worse than a milder one you keep.

Adding bonds usually softens the swings and trims long-run growth.

Fact. That is the trade, and it runs both ways. Bonds have historically steadied a portfolio while lowering its long-run growth potential — you are buying a smoother ride with some of the upside.

Cash sitting in your account doesn’t count as part of your allocation.

Myth. Uninvested cash is a weight like any other. A portfolio that is 30% cash is a different allocation from one that is fully invested, whether or not you chose it deliberately.

Recap · 12 / 13

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓An asset allocation is the split itself — not the number of funds you used to build it.
✓One total market fund is already an allocation: 100% equities, weighted by company size.
✓Each asset class does a job — a growth engine or a stabilizer.
✓Funds that hold the same companies add count, not mix.
✓The stock-to-bond split shapes results more than the provider you choose.
✓A target you can sit through, written down and dated, beats a better one you'd abandon.
Your next move: write down what your current holdings actually add up to, as percentages. You can't adjust a mix you've never measured.
