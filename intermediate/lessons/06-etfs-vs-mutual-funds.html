Side By Side · 5 / 10

Five Differences That Matter

Five rows, two wrappers. Open each one to see what it means for you rather than for the fund.

When you get a price
ETFWhenever you trade
Mutual FundOnce, at the 4 PM NAV

Intraday trading cuts both ways. You can act on a price you can see, and you can also talk yourself into trading when nothing needed doing. A fund that prices once a day quietly removes that option.

What it takes to get in
ETFOne share — or a fraction
Mutual FundOften a dollar minimum

VFIAX asks for $3,000 to open a position. VOO asks for the price of one share, and many brokerages will sell you a sliver of that. For someone starting with $100, the wrapper is the whole decision.

What you pay to transact
ETFThe bid-ask spread
Mutual FundUsually nothing

You buy a mutual fund from the fund itself, at one price, so there is no gap to cross. On a big, liquid ETF the spread is pennies. On a small one it can quietly cost more than a year of fees.

What it charges each year
ETFVOO: 0.03%
Mutual FundVFIAX: 0.04%

One basis point apart — a dollar a year on $10,000. The expense ratio is set by how the fund is run, not by which wrapper it comes in, which is why SPY costs three times VOO while holding the same index.

Automatic contributions
ETFDepends on your broker
Mutual FundBuilt for it

A mutual fund takes an exact dollar amount every payday without being asked twice. ETFs trade in shares, so $200 rarely divides cleanly — some brokerages handle it with fractional shares, and some still don't.

Myth Or Fact · 6 / 10

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims you will hear about these two wrappers. Tap your answer.

ETFs are always the cheaper of the two.

Myth. VOO charges 0.03% and VFIAX charges 0.04% for the same index — a rounding error. Meanwhile SPY, an ETF, charges more than both. The wrapper does not set the expense ratio.

A mutual fund order placed at 10 AM fills at the 10 AM price.

Myth. Everyone who ordered that day gets the same NAV, worked out after the close. Ordering at 10 AM and ordering at 3:59 PM land on the identical price.

You can buy an ETF at any moment the market is open.

Fact. Intraday trading is what the wrapper is for. It behaves like a stock on the exchange, which is a convenience and a temptation in equal measure.

Two funds in different wrappers can be the same fund underneath.

Fact. VOO and VFIAX are two share classes of one Vanguard fund. Same portfolio, same manager, same holdings — different ticker, minimum and wrapper. The next slide takes this apart.

The bid-ask spread is a fee the fund company charges you.

Myth. It is the gap between what buyers are bidding and what sellers are asking, and it goes to whoever took the other side of your trade. The fund never sees a cent of it.

The Twist · 7 / 10

Sometimes It Is Literally The Same Fund

Two tickers, two wrappers, two sets of rules. Tap each to see what is actually underneath.

Tap BothOne of these you buy on an exchange. The other you buy from the fund company. Look at what you end up owning.
The Twist

They are not two competing funds. They are two share classes of the same Vanguard 500 Index Fund, holding one shared portfolio. Vanguard will convert VFIAX into VOO without triggering a taxable sale — because nothing is really being sold.

So when someone asks whether ETFs beat mutual funds, the honest answer is that the question is often about packaging. What you own is the index. What the wrapper changes is how you get in, how little you can start with, and what you pay on the way through the door.

Recap · 9 / 10

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in five lines.

✓An ETF prices continuously, so intraday trading is possible. A mutual fund prices once, and every order that day fills at the same NAV.
✓The expense ratio is set by how a fund is run, not by its wrapper — SPY charges three times VOO for the same 500 companies.
✓ETF trades cross a bid-ask spread. It is pennies on a big fund and quietly expensive on a small one.
✓Mutual funds take exact dollar amounts and often demand a minimum; ETFs take shares and start at one.
✓VOO and VFIAX are share classes of a single fund, so "which is better" is often a question about packaging.
Your next move: look up the expense ratio and minimum of a fund you already own. Both are published, and the wrapper will be named right next to them.
