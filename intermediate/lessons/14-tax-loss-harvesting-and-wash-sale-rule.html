Myth Or Fact · 5 / 8

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims you'll hear about harvesting a loss. Tap your answer.

The wash-sale clock only looks backward at what you bought before the sale.

Myth. The window runs both directions — 30 days before the sale and 30 days after, 61 days in total including the sale date itself.

A disallowed wash-sale loss isn't gone forever — it gets added to the cost basis of the replacement shares.

Fact. The replacement shares inherit the disallowed amount as extra basis, and the original holding period carries over too. The benefit is deferred, not erased.

Selling at a loss and buying a completely different company in a different industry can trigger a wash sale.

Myth. The wash-sale rule only reaches securities the IRS would treat as substantially identical. A different company in a different industry is clearly outside that.

Up to $3,000 of net capital losses can offset ordinary income each year, with any excess carried forward to future years.

Fact. Losses offset gains first without limit, then up to $3,000 chips away at ordinary income. That $3,000 cap hasn't changed since 1978.

Buying the same stock back inside your IRA avoids the wash-sale rule since it's a different account.

Myth. It still counts — and it's worse. There's no basis adjustment available inside an IRA, so the loss is permanently forfeited rather than just delayed.

Recap · 7 / 8

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓A loss only counts on your taxes once it's realized — sold, not just sitting on paper as an unrealized loss.
✓Losses offset gains first, with no limit, then up to $3,000 a year against ordinary income.
✓Anything left over becomes a capital loss carryover into future years, with no expiration.
✓The wash-sale rule disallows the loss if you buy the same or a substantially identical security within 30 days before or after the sale.
✓A disallowed loss isn't lost forever — it rolls into the cost basis of the replacement shares.
✓Buying the replacement inside an IRA doesn't dodge the rule — it makes the loss permanently disallowed instead.
Your next move: if you're ever considering this, check the 61-day window against your own trade history first. A tax professional can confirm your specific case.
