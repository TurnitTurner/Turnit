Myth Or Fact · 5 / 10

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims about portfolio structure. Tap your answer.

More funds means a more sophisticated portfolio.

Myth. Complexity is a cost, not a credential. Extra funds are worth holding only if each one adds exposure you don't already have — otherwise you've bought more admin.

A target-date fund holds an entire portfolio inside one ticker.

Fact. Stocks, bonds, domestic and international all sit inside it, and the mix shifts along a published glide path. One holding on your statement, a full portfolio underneath.

A one-fund portfolio can't match your own risk tolerance.

Myth. You can choose a fund whose mix suits you, including one aimed at a different year than your actual retirement. What you give up is fine-tuning, not suitability.

The simplest model you'll actually stick with usually beats the clever one you won't.

Fact. Structures fail through neglect far more often than through design. A portfolio you maintain beats a better one you abandon in year three.

Core and satellite means half your money sits in bets.

Myth. The core is the point — it's meant to be most of the portfolio. Satellites are deliberately small, sized so that being wrong is an irritation rather than a disaster.

Recap · 9 / 10

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in five lines.

✓All three models can hold the same allocation. They differ in who does the work.
✓A target-date fund is a whole portfolio in one holding, moving along a published glide path.
✓A three-fund portfolio buys you control over every weight, and hands you the upkeep.
✓Core and satellite only works while the satellites stay small.
✓Each model fails differently: mismatch, neglect, or drift. Know which one is yours.
