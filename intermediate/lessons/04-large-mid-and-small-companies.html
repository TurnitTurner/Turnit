Myth Or Fact · 6 / 10

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims about company size. Tap your answer.

Small companies always grow faster than large ones.

Myth. Small companies have more room to grow, which is not the same as growing. Many never scale, some fail outright, and the tier's returns lean heavily on a minority that do work out.

A cap-weighted total market fund is dominated by its largest holdings.

Fact. Owning thousands of companies sounds spread out, but weighting by size means the biggest names drive most of the movement. Breadth of holdings is not the same as breadth of exposure.

Large-cap means safe.

Myth. Large companies tend to move less violently, but size is no shield. Plenty of household names have fallen hard, and a few have disappeared entirely.

Small-cap funds typically swing more than large-cap funds.

Fact. Smaller companies are more sensitive to borrowing costs, single customers and one bad quarter. That shows up as deeper falls and sharper recoveries.

Buying a small-cap fund is how you find the next big company early.

Myth. A fund buys the whole tier, winners and failures together. You are buying the average outcome of hundreds of small companies, not an early stake in one.

Recap · 9 / 10

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in five lines.

✓Market cap sorts companies into tiers, and the cut-offs are conventions rather than rules.
✓The tiers are nothing like equal: large-caps are roughly three-quarters of US market value.
✓A cap-weighted fund gives you that same lopsided mix automatically.
✓Smaller companies swing harder in both directions — more room to grow, more that can break.
✓Whether a small-cap tilt pays is genuinely unsettled, and anyone certain about it is overselling.
✓Tilting large, tilting small, or staying at market weight are each a real choice — there's no "correct" direction, only trade-offs.
Your next move: look up the size breakdown of a fund you already own. Most providers publish it, and most people have never looked.
