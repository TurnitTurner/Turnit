Myth Or Fact · 5 / 8

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims you'll hear about asset location. Tap your answer.

Asset location changes how much risk your portfolio is taking.

Myth. Location only changes which account holds each piece of your mix — not the mix itself. Move the exact same 70/30 stock-bond split between a taxable account and an IRA, and the risk is unchanged.

A bond fund is generally a better fit for a taxable account than for an IRA.

Myth. It's the opposite. A bond fund's interest is taxed every year it's held, in any account — except that in a tax-deferred or tax-exempt account, that yearly bill simply doesn't happen. Taxable accounts are usually the worse fit for a bond fund, not the better one.

Most REIT dividends are taxed as ordinary income, not the lower qualified rate.

Fact. Nareit's data on 2025 REIT distributions found about 79% were taxed as ordinary income. REITs pass nearly all their profit straight through instead of paying corporate tax themselves, which is also why the dividends they pay don't usually qualify for the lower rate.

Putting municipal bonds in a Roth IRA is a smart tax move.

Myth. Municipal bond interest is already exempt from federal tax in a regular taxable account. Putting it in a Roth spends limited tax-advantaged space protecting income that was never going to be taxed anyway.

Moving a holding from a taxable account into an IRA usually means selling it first.

Fact. Outside a few narrow in-kind transfer rules, you can't just move a holding between accounts — you sell it in the taxable account first. That sale can trigger capital gains tax right then, even though the goal was reducing future tax.

Recap · 7 / 8

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓Asset location is which account holds an investment — a separate decision from asset allocation, which is what you own.
✓Tax-inefficient holdings, like bond funds and REITs, generate income that's taxed every year, in any account you hold them.
✓Tax-advantaged accounts shelter that yearly bill. Tax-efficient holdings, like broad stock index funds, don't need the shelter as much.
✓Most REIT dividends are taxed as ordinary income, not the lower qualified rate — about 79% of 2025 distributions, per Nareit.
✓Municipal bonds are already tax-exempt, so putting them in a Roth wastes space that could shelter something actually taxable.
✓Moving a holding from taxable into an IRA usually means selling first, which can trigger its own tax bill.
Your next move: check where your bond funds and any REIT holdings actually sit. If they're in a taxable account and you also have room in an IRA or 401(k), that's worth a second look.
