Myth Or Fact · 7 / 13

Myth Or Fact?

Six claims about the split. Tap your answer.

Bonds are the safe part, so they can't lose money.

Myth. Bond prices fall when interest rates rise, and 2022 showed that a bond fund can post a meaningfully negative year. They are steadier than stocks, not immune.

Your split matters more in a crash than in a boom.

Fact. In a rising market almost any mix looks fine. The split earns its keep in the fall, when it decides how much you lose and whether you stay invested.

You should pick the highest stock percentage you can tolerate.

Myth. Tolerance is only half of it. If the money is needed in three years, what you can stomach is irrelevant — your capacity for loss is what binds.

Holding bonds usually costs you some long-run growth.

Fact. That is the deal being struck. You give up part of the expected return to make the ride survivable — worth it if it keeps you invested, wasted if you'd have held on anyway.

Bonds are only worth holding once you’re close to retirement.

Myth. Bonds do a job at any age: they steady the portfolio and give you something to spend from that isn’t down 30%. How much you hold changes with your situation, but "none until you’re 60" is a rule of convenience, not of reasoning.

Two people the same age can correctly hold very different splits.

Fact. Same age, different timelines, incomes, and temperaments. Age is one input among several, not the answer.

Recap · 12 / 13

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓Growth and drawdown move together. You cannot buy one without the other.
✓Risk capacity is arithmetic — your timeline and what the money is for.
✓Risk tolerance is temperament, and the lower of the two should win.
✓Bonds steady the ride and cost some long-run growth. That's the trade, not a flaw.
✓Age-based rules are a sketch. They know nothing about your job or your nerves.
✓The best split is the one you'd still be holding at the bottom.
Your next move: write your split and your rule in the same place you wrote your target. A decision made calmly is only useful if you can find it later.
