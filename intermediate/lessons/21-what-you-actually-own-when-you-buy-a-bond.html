Myth Or Fact · 5 / 8

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims you'll hear about bonds. Tap your answer.

A bondholder is a part-owner of the company, just like a stockholder.

Myth. A bondholder is a creditor — someone the company owes money to — not an owner. That's exactly why bondholders get paid before stockholders if the company runs out of money.

If a company goes bankrupt, bondholders are generally paid before stockholders.

Fact. That's seniority — creditors, including bondholders, rank ahead of owners under the standard payment-priority order.

A bond's face value is always the same as what you'd pay to buy it.

Myth. Face value is fixed — what you'd actually pay to buy the bond can be higher or lower, and moves with interest rates. More on exactly why next lesson.

A credit rating below BBB- (S&P) or Baa3 (Moody's) is considered speculative, or "junk."

Fact. That's the standard investment-grade cutoff both major rating agencies use — anything below it is speculative-grade.

U.S. Treasury bonds are generally considered to carry less credit risk than municipal or corporate bonds.

Fact. The federal government can tax and create currency to meet its obligations — a capability no state, city, or company has.

Recap · 7 / 8

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓A bond's face value is the amount it promises to repay at maturity — usually $1,000 — and isn't necessarily what you'd pay to buy it.
✓A bondholder is a creditor, not an owner — which is exactly why bondholders are generally paid before stockholders if a company runs out of money.
✓Credit risk is the risk the issuer can't pay — separate from the interest-rate risk that affects a bond's market price regardless of the issuer's health.
✓A credit rating below BBB-/Baa3 is speculative-grade, commonly called "junk" — everything above it is investment-grade.
✓Payment priority in a bankruptcy generally runs secured debt, then unsecured debt, then preferred stock, then common stock.
✓Who's actually borrowing — the federal government, a state or city, or a company — is the single biggest driver of a bond's credit risk.
Your next move: before buying any bond or bond fund, check who's actually borrowing and what they're rated. The coupon rate alone never tells you that.
