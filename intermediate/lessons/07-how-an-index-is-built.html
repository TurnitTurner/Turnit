When It Changes · 4 / 8

One Rule Change, Four Consequences

A company clears every test. Tap each link in the chain to follow what happens next.

The committee meets, applies the tests and votes. No one argues about whether the business is any good.

The change is published about five trading days before it takes effect. Everybody finds out at the same time.

Every fund tracking that index has to hold it. They have no choice — tracking the index is the whole job.

Traders can see the buying coming, so the stock often moves before a single fund manager places an order.

Roughly $9 trillion is benchmarked to Russell indexes alone. Their yearly reconstitution, the last Friday of June, is routinely one of the heaviest trading days of the year.

Myth Or Fact · 6 / 8

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims about how indexes work. Tap your answer.

An index is a list someone picks by hand each year.

Myth. Nearly all of them run on published rules. Even the S&P 500, which does have a nine-person committee, applies fixed tests before anyone votes — and Russell's indexes use no discretion at all.

A fund buys more of a company because it got bigger, not because it got better.

Fact. Cap-weighting follows price. When a stock doubles, its weight roughly doubles, and the fund holds more of it. Nobody judged the business.

The S&P 500 will take any big enough company.

Myth. It also wants profits — positive earnings last quarter and across the last four combined — plus a free float of at least 10%. Size is one test of several.

Two funds holding the same 500 companies must perform the same.

Myth. VOO and RSP hold identical companies in very different proportions. Same names, different weighting, and they do not move together.

An index change can move a stock before any fund trades it.

Fact. Changes are published days ahead and the money that must follow is enormous, so the price often adjusts while the funds are still waiting for the effective date.

Recap · 7 / 8

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓An index is a rulebook, not a shopping list.
✓Three rules do all the work: who gets in, how much each gets, when it changes.
✓Eligibility rules are more than size — the S&P 500 wants profits and a real free float.
✓Weighting decides where your money lands: VOO's top ten is about 38.6% of the fund, while RSP gives each of 500 companies roughly 0.20%.
✓Reconstitution runs on a published calendar, so a fund's trades surprise nobody.
✓Trillions follow these rules, which is why a rule change moves real money.
Your next move: find the words "tracks the ___ Index" on a fund you own, then look up that index's rules. They are published, and almost nobody reads them.
