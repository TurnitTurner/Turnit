Myth Or Fact · 5 / 8

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims you'll hear about bond pricing. Tap your answer.

A bond's coupon rate changes as interest rates change, so its payment always stays competitive.

Myth. The coupon rate is fixed for the bond's entire life. It's the price that adjusts to keep the bond competitive, not the coupon.

Current yield and yield to maturity are usually the exact same number.

Myth. They're only equal when a bond trades at exactly par. Anywhere else, they diverge — and yield to maturity is the more complete number.

When interest rates rise, prices on existing, fixed-coupon bonds tend to fall.

Fact. That's interest rate risk — new bonds pay more, so an older, lower-coupon bond's price has to drop to stay competitive.

A bond trading at a premium means something is wrong with the issuer.

Myth. A premium is usually just about timing — the bond's fixed coupon happens to beat what new bonds are paying today. It says nothing about credit risk on its own.

Yield to maturity accounts for the gap between a bond's purchase price and its face value, not just its coupon payments.

Fact. That gap — a built-in gain or loss by maturity — is exactly what current yield leaves out and yield to maturity captures.

Recap · 7 / 8

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓A bond's coupon rate and face value never change after it's issued — its market price is what moves.
✓When interest rates rise, existing fixed-coupon bond prices fall. When rates fall, those prices rise. That's interest rate risk.
✓Current yield is just the coupon divided by price. Yield to maturity is the more complete number — it also accounts for any gap between price and face value.
✓A bond trades at par, a premium, or a discount depending only on how its fixed coupon compares to what new bonds are paying today.
✓A premium or a discount, by itself, says nothing about an issuer's health — that's credit risk, a separate question entirely.
✓This lesson covered direction only. How much a given rate move actually moves a price — and why that differs bond to bond — is next.
Your next move: next time you hear a rate decision in the news, ask what it would do to a bond you already own. Up or down is the whole mechanism in one question.
