Myth Or Fact · 5 / 8

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims you'll hear about your 1099. Tap your answer.

If box 1e (cost basis) is blank, you don't need to figure out your own cost basis.

Myth. A blank box usually means the broker wasn't required to track it — often shares bought before 2012. You still need your own purchase records.

A single broker can send you one consolidated 1099 that bundles dividend, interest, and sale information together.

Fact. Most brokerages mail one combined document instead of three separate forms — the sections inside still carry each form's own name and box numbers.

Box 1a (total ordinary dividends) and box 1b (qualified dividends) are always two completely different numbers.

Myth. Box 1b is a slice of box 1a, not a separate total — the two can even be equal if every dividend you were paid happened to qualify.

A wash-sale adjustment on your 1099-B doesn't erase the loss — it rolls into the cost basis of the shares you bought back.

Fact. The disallowed amount becomes extra basis on the replacement shares, so the benefit is deferred rather than gone for good.

You can just copy the numbers from your 1099 onto your return without double-checking them.

Myth. Brokers sometimes issue corrected 1099s after the original. Checking the numbers against your own records catches errors before they become your problem.

Recap · 7 / 8

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓A consolidated 1099 bundles your dividend, interest, and sale reporting into one document.
✓Box 1a is total ordinary dividends; box 1b is the slice of that taxed at the lower qualified rate.
✓Form 1099-B reports proceeds and cost basis for each sale — the difference is your gain or loss.
✓Box 2 tells you short-term or long-term, which decides which rate table applies.
✓A wash-sale adjustment doesn't erase a loss — it rolls into the replacement shares' cost basis.
✓If cost basis is blank, the broker didn't track it — you still need your own records.
Your next move: pull up last year's 1099 and find boxes 1a, 1b, and 2 on an actual sale. You now know what each one is telling you.
