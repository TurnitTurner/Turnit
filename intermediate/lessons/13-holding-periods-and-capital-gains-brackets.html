Myth Or Fact · 5 / 8

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims you'll hear about holding periods and brackets. Tap your answer.

If part of your gain pushes you into the 20% bracket, your entire gain is taxed at 20%.

Myth. Only the slice of the gain above the threshold gets the higher rate. The rest stays in the lower zone it already filled.

Your ordinary income is counted first when figuring out which capital-gains bracket your gain falls into.

Fact. That's income stacking. Your wages and other ordinary income fill the brackets from zero up, and the gain is taxed on whatever room is left above that.

Reinvesting a dividend instead of taking it as cash resets the holding period on your original shares.

Myth. A reinvested dividend buys new shares with their own fresh holding period. Your original shares keep the clock they already had.

A gain is short-term if you hold the asset for one year or less, and long-term only once you've held it more than one year.

Fact. Exactly one year to the day still counts as short-term. You need at least one day past the anniversary.

Long-term losses get a special lower tax rate, the same way long-term gains do.

Myth. There's no preferential rate on a loss — a loss just offsets income. Holding period mainly matters for which gains it can offset first.

Recap · 7 / 8

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓A gain is short-term if held one year or less, long-term only once held more than one year — exactly one year still counts as short-term.
✓Short-term gains are taxed at your regular income rate. Long-term gains get the lower 0%, 15%, or 20% rate instead.
✓Your ordinary income is counted first. A long-term gain stacks on top of it when figuring out which rate zone applies.
✓A single gain can straddle two rate zones — part at 15%, part at 20% — if it crosses a threshold.
✓Only the portion of the gain above a threshold gets the higher rate — never the whole gain.
✓Reinvesting a dividend starts a new holding period only for the new shares bought — your original shares are unaffected.
Your next move: find one holding close to its one-year mark and check the date before you sell. A few extra days can change which rate applies.
