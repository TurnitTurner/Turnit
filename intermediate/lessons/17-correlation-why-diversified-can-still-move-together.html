Myth Or Fact · 5 / 8

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims you'll hear about diversification and correlation. Tap your answer.

If you own 50 different stocks, you're automatically diversified.

Myth. If all 50 are in the same industry, they likely share the same exposure and move together anyway — that's sector concentration, not diversification, no matter how many tickers it is.

A correlation of 0 between two investments means they always move in opposite directions.

Myth. 0 means no relationship at all — knowing what one did tells you nothing about the other. Opposite movement is closer to -1, a very different number.

Correlation between two investments can change over time, including rising sharply during a crisis.

Fact. Plenty of investments that normally move somewhat independently have historically moved more closely together during sharp, broad sell-offs — it isn't a number stamped on an asset forever.

Idiosyncratic risk — the risk specific to one company — can be reduced by adding more, different holdings.

Fact. A bad earnings report at one company matters less to your overall portfolio the more other, genuinely different things you hold alongside it.

Systematic risk can be eliminated completely by owning enough different stocks.

Myth. Stocks alone, however many and however different, are all still exposed to the same stock-market-wide risk. Reducing it meaningfully takes genuinely different kinds of assets, not just more tickers of the same kind.

Recap · 7 / 8

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓Correlation measures how closely two investments move together, on a scale from -1 (opposite) to +1 (lockstep).
✓Owning many holdings isn't the same as diversifying — if they're all highly correlated, it's closer to one bet than many.
✓Systematic risk hits the whole market at once and isn't removed by holding more of the same asset class; idiosyncratic risk is specific to one holding and shrinks as you add genuinely different ones.
✓Correlation isn't fixed — it can shift, and has historically tended to rise during sharp, broad sell-offs.
✓In 2022, U.S. stocks and long-term Treasury bonds fell together — a reminder that even a usual relationship isn't guaranteed every year.
✓A real diversifier is something that doesn't move in lockstep with what you already own — not just another ticker.
Your next move: pick your two largest holdings and ask whether they'd fall for the same reason. If the honest answer is yes, that's sector concentration wearing a diversified costume.
