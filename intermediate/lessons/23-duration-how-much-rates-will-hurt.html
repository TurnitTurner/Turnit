Myth Or Fact · 5 / 8

Myth Or Fact?

Five claims you'll hear about duration. Tap your answer.

Duration just tells you how many years until a bond matures.

Myth. Maturity is one input, but duration also weighs every coupon payment received along the way — which is why a coupon-paying bond's duration is almost always shorter than its maturity.

A bond with a longer duration is more sensitive to interest rate changes than one with a shorter duration.

Fact. That's the entire point of the number — it ranks bonds by how hard a given rate move will hit their price.

Duration gives you an exact price change, down to the penny.

Myth. It's a straight-line approximation. The bigger the rate move, the further the estimate drifts from the actual result — that's convexity.

Two bonds can have very different credit ratings but the exact same duration.

Fact. Duration measures rate sensitivity. Credit rating measures default risk. They're entirely separate questions about the same bond.

A zero-coupon bond's duration equals its time to maturity exactly.

Fact. With no coupon payments along the way, every dollar of cash flow arrives in one lump at maturity — so there's nothing earlier to pull the duration shorter.

Recap · 7 / 8

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓Duration is a single number, in years, that estimates how sensitive a bond's price is to a change in rates — the longer the duration, the bigger the swing.
✓The same 1% rate move hits a 2-year bond and a 20-year bond very differently: the longer bond's price moved roughly six times as much.
✓Duration's estimate assumes a clean parallel shift and gets less accurate the bigger the move — that gap is convexity.
✓A lower coupon can stretch a bond's duration even at the exact same maturity, because more of its value sits further out in time.
✓Duration measures interest rate risk, not credit risk — two bonds with very different credit ratings can share the exact same duration.
✓Next: what it actually feels like to hold a bond fund through a rate shock, versus holding one bond yourself.
Your next move: before buying any bond or bond fund, find its duration first. It tells you more about what a rate move will do to you than the maturity date ever will.
