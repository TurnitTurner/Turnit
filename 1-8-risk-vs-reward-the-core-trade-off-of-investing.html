The Real Danger · 8 / 13

Myth Or Fact?

Six common beliefs about risk. Tap your answer for each — the explanation appears right after.

If you avoid the stock market entirely, your money is completely safe from all risk.

Myth. Cash sitting still faces inflation risk — it can quietly lose real buying power even while the number in the account never drops.

Historic maximum drawdown measures the worst peak-to-trough drop an investment has experienced.

Fact. That is exactly what drawdown measures, and it is one of the most useful ways to understand how rough the ride can actually get.

A properly diversified portfolio can never lose value in any given year.

Myth. Diversification reduces risk, it does not eliminate it. A well-diversified portfolio can still lose value in a broad downturn — just usually less severely.

Two investments can have the same average return and still put you through very different rides.

Fact. An average hides the path taken to get there. One investment can drift up steadily while another swings 40% in both directions and lands in the same place.

Taking on more risk is guaranteed to earn you a higher return.

Myth. More risk widens the range of possible outcomes — it does not promise a bigger one. Some risks, like betting everything on one company, add danger without adding expected reward.

Holding one company's stock exposes you to risks a broad fund would have diluted.

Fact. That is concentration risk. A bad quarter, a lawsuit, or a failed product can hit a single holding hard, while the same event barely registers inside a fund holding hundreds of companies.

Recap · 12 / 13

Here's What You Now Know

The whole lesson, in six lines.

✓Every asset has its own "pulse" — cash barely moves, while speculative assets swing wildly.
✓Higher expected return almost always comes with a deeper possible drawdown.
✓Risk isn't one thing: volatility, drawdown, concentration, and time horizon risk are all different.
✓The same volatile asset can be fine for a long-term goal and dangerous for a short-term one.
✓Diversification, dollar-cost averaging, and rebalancing manage risk — none of them eliminate it.
✓A 50% loss needs a 100% gain just to break even — losses and gains are never symmetric.
